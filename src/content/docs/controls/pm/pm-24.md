## Control statement

Establish a Data Integrity Board to:

- **a.** Review proposals to conduct or participate in a matching program; and
- **b.** Conduct an annual review of all matching programs in which the agency has participated.

<details>
<summary>NIST discussion</summary>

A Data Integrity Board is the board of senior officials designated by the head of a federal agency and is responsible for, among other things, reviewing the agency’s proposals to conduct or participate in a matching program and conducting an annual review of all matching programs in which the agency has participated. As a general matter, a matching program is a computerized comparison of records from two or more automated PRIVACT systems of records or an automated system of records and automated records maintained by a non-federal agency (or agent thereof). A matching program either pertains to Federal benefit programs or Federal personnel or payroll records. At a minimum, the Data Integrity Board includes the Inspector General of the agency, if any, and the senior agency official for privacy.

</details>

## How to apply it

PM-24 asks you to establish a data integrity board. The board reviews proposals to conduct or take part in a matching program (a), and reviews every matching program the organization took part in each year (b). PM-24 is in the SP 800-53B Privacy baseline.

NIST's PM-24 discussion describes a matching program as a computerized comparison of records from two or more automated Privacy Act systems of records, or of such a system with a non-federal agency's automated records. A matching program pertains to federal benefit programs or to federal personnel or payroll records. The board is made up of senior officials the agency head designates, and at a minimum includes the inspector general, if any, and the senior agency official for privacy.

The term and the duty come from the U.S. Privacy Act, so PM-24 applies to organizations that run or take part in matching programs. The [Program Management policy](/templates/policies/pm/) says to keep the heading and record the decision in the program plan if the organization runs none. The steps for each matching program, including the board's approval before it starts, are in [PT-8](/controls/pt/pt-8/) and the [PII Processing and Transparency policy](/templates/policies/pt/).

**Common implementations.** The PM-24 clause has the senior leader establish the board where the organization conducts or takes part in a matching program. A charter that would pass an assessment states:

- The members, by role, including the inspector general and the senior privacy official, and the chair
- A secretary who keeps the board's records
- How proposals and matching agreements reach the board, and the record of each decision (a)
- The calendar for the annual review of every matching program, and who prepares it (b)
- How the board reports its review to the senior leader and publishes it

The board's records connect to other controls. Matching agreements and notices are posted on the privacy webpage ([PM-20](/controls/pm/pm-20/)). The privacy impact assessment for each system in a match lists the match as a disclosure, and the accounting of disclosures ([PM-21](/controls/pm/pm-21/)) records it.

**Organization-defined parameters.** PM-24 has none. Its clause leaves these choices open, with typical values your organization may set differently:

| Choice | Typical value |
| --- | --- |
| Who establishes the board | The senior leader (the clause) |
| Who sits on it | The senior privacy official, the inspector general (if any, and not as chair), the general counsel, and the chief information officer |
| When the annual review is held (b) | At the end of each calendar year |
| Where the decision is recorded if the organization runs no matching programs | The program plan |

**Evidence assessors ask for.**

- The document that establishes the board, with its members and chair
- Records of the board's review and decision on each matching proposal and agreement (a)
- The last annual review of all matching programs, and how it was reported (b)
- The list of the organization's matching programs, with their agreements and notices

**Inheritance.** PM-24 is implemented once, for the whole organization, and every system relies on it. NIST's PM-1 discussion treats program management controls as independent of any system and distinct from common controls. Each system that takes part in a matching program does so under the board's approval.

**Common findings.**

- Matching programs running with no board review, often because nobody recognized them as matching programs.
- A board whose membership lacks the inspector general or the senior privacy official.
- No annual review, or a review that lists programs without assessing them (b).
- Matching agreements renewed by staff after the board's approval expired.

**Enhancements.** PM-24 has no enhancements.

**Federal systems** (as of October 2026). Every agency conducting or participating in a matching program "shall establish a Data Integrity Board" ([5 U.S.C. § 552a(u)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/5/552a?link-type=html)). The agency head designates its senior officials, including the official responsible for the Privacy Act and the inspector general, if any, who may not chair it. The board reviews, approves and maintains all written matching agreements, and reviews each year all matching programs the agency took part in. It also compiles an annual report for the agency head and OMB, available to the public on request. It does not approve an agreement without a cost-benefit analysis showing the program is likely to be cost effective, unless one of the statutory exceptions applies, under (u)(4).

[OMB Circular A-108](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/legacy_drupal_files/omb/circulars/A108/omb_circular_a-108.pdf) (December 2016), section 5.b, identifies the SAOP as a member. Section 14 has each agency's board review the year's matching programs at the end of each calendar year. The report goes to OMB by June 1 and is posted at `www.[agency].gov/privacy`, listing the members and each program's compliance with its agreement.
