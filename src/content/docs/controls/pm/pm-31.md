## Control statement

Develop an organization-wide continuous monitoring strategy and implement continuous monitoring programs that include:

- **a.** Establishing the following organization-wide metrics to be monitored: [Assignment: organization-defined metrics];
- **b.** Establishing [Assignment: organization-defined monitoring frequencies] and [Assignment: organization-defined assessment frequencies] for control effectiveness;
- **c.** Ongoing monitoring of organizationally-defined metrics in accordance with the continuous monitoring strategy;
- **d.** Correlation and analysis of information generated by control assessments and monitoring;
- **e.** Response actions to address results of the analysis of control assessment and monitoring information; and
- **f.** Reporting the security and privacy status of organizational systems to [Assignment: organization-defined personnel or roles] [Assignment: organization-defined frequency].

<details>
<summary>NIST discussion</summary>

Continuous monitoring at the organization level facilitates ongoing awareness of the security and privacy posture across the organization to support organizational risk management decisions. The terms "continuous" and "ongoing" imply that organizations assess and monitor their controls and risks at a frequency sufficient to support risk-based decisions. Different types of controls may require different monitoring frequencies. The results of continuous monitoring guide and inform risk response actions by organizations. Continuous monitoring programs allow organizations to maintain the authorizations of systems and common controls in highly dynamic environments of operation with changing mission and business needs, threats, vulnerabilities, and technologies. Having access to security- and privacy-related information on a continuing basis through reports and dashboards gives organizational officials the capability to make effective, timely, and informed risk management decisions, including ongoing authorization decisions. To further facilitate security and privacy risk management, organizations consider aligning organization-defined monitoring metrics with organizational risk tolerance as defined in the risk management strategy. Monitoring requirements, including the need for monitoring, may be referenced in other controls and control enhancements such as, AC-2g, AC-2(7), AC-2(12)(a), AC-2(7)(b), AC-2(7)(c), AC-17(1), AT-4a, AU-13, AU-13(1), AU-13(2), CA-7, CM-3f, CM-6d, CM-11c, IR-5, MA-2b, MA-3a, MA-4a, PE-3d, PE-6, PE-14b, PE-16, PE-20, PM-6, PM-23, PS-7e, SA-9c, SC-5(3)(b), SC-7a, SC-7(24)(b), SC-18b, SC-43b, SI-4.

## How to apply it

PM-31 asks for an organization-wide continuous monitoring strategy, and continuous monitoring programs that follow it. The programs set organization-wide metrics (a) and frequencies for monitoring and for assessing control effectiveness (b). They monitor the metrics on an ongoing basis (c), correlate and analyze what assessments and monitoring produce (d), act on the results (e), and report the security and privacy status of systems to set roles at a set frequency (f). PM-31 is in the SP 800-53B Privacy baseline.

NIST's PM-31 discussion says organization-level monitoring gives ongoing awareness of the security and privacy posture to support risk decisions. "Continuous" and "ongoing" mean often enough to support risk-based decisions, and different controls may need different frequencies. Monitoring lets organizations keep authorizations of systems and common controls current, including ongoing authorization, and the metrics should align with the risk tolerance in the risk management strategy ([PM-9](/controls/pm/pm-9/)). Each system's monitoring ([CA-7](/controls/ca/ca-7/)) works within it.

[SP 800-37 Rev. 2](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/37/r2/final) task P-7 asks for the same strategy, with the senior accountable official for risk management or the risk executive (function) ([PM-29](/controls/pm/pm-29/)) primarily responsible. The strategy sets the minimum monitoring frequency for implemented controls, the ongoing assessment approach, and may set reporting requirements and recipients. That official or function "approves the continuous monitoring strategy including the minimum frequency with which controls are to be monitored." [SP 800-137](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/137/final) (September 2011, current as of October 2026) describes the process: define the strategy, establish the program (section 3.2.1 on metrics, 3.2.2 on frequencies), implement it, analyze and report, respond, and review and update. The P-7 discussion says the strategy can include supply chain risk, such as regular reviews of suppliers ([PM-30](/controls/pm/pm-30/)).

**Common implementations.** The [Continuous Monitoring Strategy](/templates/plans/continuous-monitoring-strategy/) template holds both levels. Part A is the organization-wide strategy: the metrics, monitoring and assessment frequencies and reporting (section 3), the minimum frequencies every system meets (section 4), and the tools (section 5). Part B is each system's strategy under CA-7. Sections 10 to 13 carry out analysis, reporting, response and review. The [Program Management policy](/templates/policies/pm/) has the Chief Information Security Officer develop the strategy and report security status, and the senior privacy official report privacy status. Section 14 records the approval of Part A by the senior accountable official for risk management, as task P-7 describes, and by the senior privacy official for the privacy controls.

The strategy covers privacy controls too. The template lets one document serve as both the security and the privacy continuous monitoring strategy; if your organization keeps them apart, say in Part A where the privacy strategy is.

Correlation and analysis (d) is where many programs fall short. Look across systems for the same weakness, compare metrics against their thresholds and trends, and feed the results into common control changes and plans of action and milestones ([PM-4](/controls/pm/pm-4/), [CA-5](/controls/ca/ca-5/)).

**Organization-defined parameters.** From `templates/policy/pm/`. Typical values, which your organization may set differently:

| Parameter | Typical value |
| --- | --- |
| Organization-wide metrics (a) | Systems with a current authorization, plan of action items past due, vulnerabilities remediated within the required times, and staff with current security awareness training |
| Monitoring frequencies (b) | Continuously for automated controls, and at least monthly for vulnerability, configuration and account data |
| Assessment frequencies (b) | A third of each system's controls each year, so every control is assessed at least every three years |
| Roles that receive security status (f) | The senior leader and the authorizing officials |
| Roles that receive privacy status (f) | The senior leader and the authorizing officials |
| Security status reporting frequency (f) | Monthly |
| Privacy status reporting frequency (f) | Quarterly |

The metrics match the performance measures in section 6 of the [Information Security Program Plan](/templates/plans/information-security-program-plan/), so one data set serves both. SP 800-137 section 3.2.1 gives similar examples, such as vulnerabilities found and remediated and employees current on awareness training.

**Evidence assessors ask for.**

- The approved strategy, with the approval of the senior accountable official for risk management or the risk executive (function)
- Definitions of each metric, with its calculation, data source and threshold (a)
- The monitoring and assessment schedules, and records that they were followed (b, c)
- Analysis outputs, such as dashboards and trend reports (d)
- Response records: plans of action and milestones, changed controls, changed frequencies (e)
- Security and privacy status reports, with dates and recipients (f)
- The record of the last review and update of the strategy

**Inheritance.** PM-31 is implemented once, for the whole organization, and every system relies on it. NIST's PM-1 discussion treats program management controls as independent of any system and distinct from common controls. Each system's continuous monitoring (CA-7) follows the organization-wide strategy and may tighten it but not loosen it.

**Common findings.**

- Metrics defined in the strategy but never collected.
- Frequencies set for automated controls only, with nothing for controls assessed by hand.
- Status reports that go to the Chief Information Security Officer and no further (f).
- No privacy status reporting, or privacy controls left out of the strategy.
- Findings from monitoring that never reach a plan of action and milestones (e).
- A strategy approved only by its author, with no senior official approving the frequencies.

**Enhancements.** PM-31 has no enhancements.

**Federal systems** (as of October 2026). [OMB Circular A-130](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/legacy_drupal_files/omb/circulars/A130/a130revised.pdf), Appendix I, section 4.d(5) to (8), requires an information security continuous monitoring (ISCM) strategy and program, with metrics that show security status and trends at all risk management tiers. Section 4.d(9) and (10) require a privacy continuous monitoring (PCM) strategy that assigns an agency-defined assessment frequency to each privacy control, and a PCM program, and footnote 85 allows the two to be combined. Section 5.k says the strategies must document all controls selected and implemented, including the frequency and rigor of monitoring, and all common controls inherited by agency systems; they "must be approved by the appropriate agency authorizing official and SAOP, respectively." OMB's [FY 2026 SAOP FISMA Reporting Metrics](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/FY26-saop-fisma-metrics.pdf) (August 2026) ask SAOPs to submit the privacy continuous monitoring strategy through CyberScope by October 30, 2026.
