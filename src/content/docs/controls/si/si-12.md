## Control statement

Manage and retain information within the system and information output from the system in accordance with applicable laws, executive orders, directives, regulations, policies, standards, guidelines and operational requirements.

<details>
<summary>NIST discussion</summary>

Information management and retention requirements cover the full life cycle of information, in some cases extending beyond system disposal. Information to be retained may also include policies, procedures, plans, reports, data output from control implementation, and other types of administrative information. The National Archives and Records Administration (NARA) provides federal policy and guidance on records retention and schedules. If organizations have a records management office, consider coordinating with records management personnel. Records produced from the output of implemented controls that may require management and retention include, but are not limited to: All XX-1, AC-6(9), AT-4, AU-12, CA-2, CA-3, CA-5, CA-6, CA-7, CA-8, CA-9, CM-2, CM-3, CM-4, CM-6, CM-8, CM-9, CM-12, CM-13, CP-2, IR-6, IR-8, MA-2, MA-4, PE-2, PE-8, PE-16, PE-17, PL-2, PL-4, PL-7, PL-8, PM-5, PM-8, PM-9, PM-18, PM-21, PM-27, PM-28, PM-30, PM-31, PS-2, PS-6, PS-7, PT-2, PT-3, PT-7, RA-2, RA-3, RA-5, RA-8, SA-4, SA-5, SA-8, SA-10, SI-4, SR-2, SR-4, SR-8.

### SI-12(1) Limit Personally Identifiable Information Elements

*Baselines: Privacy*

Limit personally identifiable information being processed in the information life cycle to the following elements of personally identifiable information: [Assignment: organization-defined elements of personally identifiable information].

<details>
<summary>Discussion and assessment objectives for SI-12(1)</summary>

The information life cycle includes information creation, collection, use, processing, storage, maintenance, dissemination, disclosure, and disposition. ## How to apply it

SI-12 asks you to manage and retain information in the system, and information the system outputs, as laws, executive orders, directives, regulations, policies, standards, guidelines and operational requirements require. NIST's SI-12 discussion says these requirements cover the full life cycle of information, sometimes beyond the system's disposal, and that information to retain includes policies, procedures, plans, reports and the output of controls. It lists many controls whose records may need retaining, including every -1 control, the CA assessments and authorizations, the CM baselines and change records, CP-2, IR-6, IR-8, RA-3, RA-5 and SI-4. It says NARA provides federal policy and guidance on records retention and schedules, and suggests coordinating with records management staff. SI-12 is in the Low, Moderate and High baselines and the Privacy baseline.

SI-12 has two sides: keeping information as long as required, and getting rid of it when the period ends. Assessors check both, and the second is where systems usually fall short, because nothing breaks when old data is kept.

**Common implementations.** The organization's records retention schedule, owned by records management, sets a period for each type of record. Each system security plan, and the [privacy impact assessment](/templates/reports/privacy-impact-assessment/) for personally identifiable information, names the types of information the system holds and the period and authority for each. The system enforces the periods with retention settings in its storage, database, log platform and backup tool, and automated jobs that delete expired records. Legal holds suspend deletion for the records they cover, through the same tools. Each year the system owner confirms that disposal happened, including for backups, exports and copies held by service providers.

**Organization-defined parameters.** SI-12 has no parameters. The [System and Information Integrity policy](/templates/policies/si/) has the system owner manage and retain information as the control states, document the retention period and its authority for each type of information the system holds, drawn from the organization's records retention schedule, and dispose of information when its period ends unless a legal hold or an approved extension applies. Its [decision worksheet](/templates/worksheets/si/) asks where retention periods are set and who confirms disposal; the typical answer is the records retention schedule, with the system owner confirming disposal each year. Two other policies depend on SI-12: media are sanitized only after confirming no retention requirement or legal hold still applies ([MP-6](/controls/mp/mp-6/), recorded in the [media sanitization record](/templates/forms/media-sanitization-record/)), and audit records are kept for the [AU-11](/controls/au/au-11/) period.

**Evidence assessors ask for.**

- The records retention schedule, and the types of information the system holds mapped to it, with periods and authorities
- Retention settings in the database, storage, log platform and backup tool, compared with the periods
- Records of the deletion jobs that ran, or of disposal confirmed in the last year
- How legal holds are applied and released
- For a sample of record types, the oldest records still held, compared with the period

**Inheritance.** The retention schedule and the records management program are common controls. The system owns applying them: setting the periods in its own tools and disposing of its own information. A service provider that stores the system's information must support the periods and delete on request or at contract end; the contract and the [system security plan](/templates/plans/system-security-plan/) should say how.

**Common findings.**

- No retention period documented for the system's information, or "keep forever" by default.
- Records deleted in the application but still in backups, exports or a provider's storage years later.
- Retention settings in the log platform that do not match the AU-11 period or the schedule.
- Disposal stopped by a legal hold that ended long ago.
- Information output from the system, such as reports and extracts on shared drives, outside any retention control.

**Enhancements in the Moderate baseline.** None. SI-12 has three enhancements, all in the Privacy baseline only.

**Enhancements in the Privacy baseline.** Each applies to a system that processes personally identifiable information, and each is recorded in the privacy impact assessment.

- **[SI-12(1)](#si-12.1)** limits the personally identifiable information processed across the information life cycle to the elements you name. NIST's discussion says limiting information not needed for operational purposes reduces privacy risk, and that risk assessments and applicable laws, regulations and policies help determine which elements create risk. Typical value: the data elements the system's privacy impact assessment lists as needed for its stated purposes. Its section 3 lists each element with why it is needed, and its minimization entry records the elements considered and left out.
- **[SI-12(2)](#si-12.2)** minimizes personally identifiable information in research, testing and training. NIST's discussion names de-identification and synthetic data as techniques. Typical values: for research, de-identified or synthetic data, or where the research needs identifiable data, only the elements the senior privacy official approves; for testing, synthetic data, or masked copies of production data; for training, fictitious or synthetic records. The SA-11 clause has developers test with data prepared under SI-12(2), not live personally identifiable information, unless the senior privacy official approves; the organization-wide rules are under [PM-25](/controls/pm/pm-25/). NIST SP 800-188, [De-Identifying Government Datasets: Techniques and Governance](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/188/final) (September 2023), the final version as of October 2026, describes de-identification techniques, including synthetic data, and their governance.
- **[SI-12(3)](#si-12.3)** sets the techniques used to dispose of, destroy or erase information after the retention period. NIST's discussion says disposal applies to originals, copies and archived records, including system logs that may contain personally identifiable information. Typical values: to dispose of it, deletion by the system's automated retention jobs, and shredding for paper output; to destroy it, destruction of storage media by shredding, disintegration or incineration, as the media sanitization procedure sets out; to erase it, cryptographic erase or overwriting, as the media sanitization procedure sets out. The media techniques follow NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2, [Guidelines for Media Sanitization](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/88/r2/final) (September 2025), the final version as of October 2026, under [MP-6](/controls/mp/mp-6/). Section 7 of the privacy impact assessment records the disposal method for each record.

**Federal systems** (as of October 2026). The head of each federal agency must "make and preserve records containing adequate and proper documentation" of the agency's organization, functions, policies, decisions, procedures and essential transactions ([44 U.S.C. § 3101](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/44/3101?link-type=html)), and federal records "may not be alienated or destroyed except under" chapter 33 of title 44 ([44 U.S.C. § 3314](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/44/3314?link-type=html)), which includes the General Records Schedules the Archivist issues for records common to agencies ([44 U.S.C. § 3303a(d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/44/3303a?link-type=html)); text checked in the United States Code, 2024 edition. NARA's [General Records Schedules](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/grs) page says "Use of the GRS is mandatory. Agencies must use the GRS unless they can justify the use of an agency-specific schedule." The SI-12 clause's federal block has the system owner retain and dispose of federal records in the system only as an approved agency records schedule or a General Records Schedule allows. Work out the periods with the agency records officer.
