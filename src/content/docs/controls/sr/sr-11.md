## Control statement

- **a.** Develop and implement anti-counterfeit policy and procedures that include the means to detect and prevent counterfeit components from entering the system; and
- **b.** Report counterfeit system components to [Selection (one or more): source of counterfeit component; [Assignment: organization-defined external reporting organizations] ; [Assignment: organization-defined personnel or roles] ].

<details>
<summary>NIST discussion</summary>

Sources of counterfeit components include manufacturers, developers, vendors, and contractors. Anti-counterfeiting policies and procedures support tamper resistance and provide a level of protection against the introduction of malicious code. External reporting organizations include CISA.

</details>

## How to apply it

SR-11 asks for an anti-counterfeit policy and procedures that include the means to detect counterfeit components and keep them out of the system (a), and for reporting counterfeit components to the source of the component, to external reporting organizations, to people inside the organization, or any combination (b). NIST's SR-11 discussion names manufacturers, developers, vendors and contractors as sources of counterfeit components, says anti-counterfeit policies and procedures support tamper resistance and protect against the introduction of malicious code, and names CISA as an external reporting organization. SR-11 is in the Low, Moderate and High baselines.

[NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/161/r1/upd1/final), Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management Practices for Systems and Organizations (May 2022, updated November 1, 2024; current as of October 2026), adds in its Appendix A guidance for SR-11 that the policy and procedures need input from acquisition, information technology, IT security, legal and the supply chain risk management office. They should address regulatory requirements, contract clauses, and counterfeit reporting processes, such as the Government-Industry Data Exchange Program (GIDEP), and, where appropriate, qualified bidders or qualified manufacturers lists, which keep purchases with authorized suppliers.

The anti-counterfeit policy is the SR-11 statements of the [Supply Chain Risk Management policy](/templates/policies/sr/); the procedure sets out the checks for each type of component. Buying from authorized sources is the strongest control, which is why the same rule appears in SR-5.

**Common implementations.** Hardware bought only from the original manufacturer, its authorized distributors or resellers, or a source the supply chain risk management team has approved. A receipt check of part and serial numbers, labels and packaging against the order and the manufacturer's identifiers, under [PE-16](/controls/pe/pe-16/), recorded in the [component inventory](/templates/forms/component-inventory/). Software, firmware and updates downloaded from the manufacturer's own site or repository and verified by digital signature or published hash before installation, usually automatically by the package manager, the endpoint management tool or the build pipeline. A marked quarantine shelf or cage for suspect components. A counterfeit found in a running system is handled as an incident under the [incident response plan](/templates/plans/incident-response-plan/).

**Organization-defined parameters.** Typical values, from the [Supply Chain Risk Management policy](/templates/policies/sr/), which your organization may set differently:

| Parameter | Typical value |
| --- | --- |
| Who counterfeit components are reported to (b) | The source of the counterfeit component, the original manufacturer and CISA, and the Chief Information Security Officer, the supply chain risk management team and the system owner |
| External reporting organizations (b) | The original manufacturer of the genuine component, and CISA |
| Personnel or roles reported to (b) | The Chief Information Security Officer, the supply chain risk management team and the system owner |
| Staff trained to detect counterfeits (SR-11(1)) | Staff who receive, inspect, install or repair hardware, procurement staff who buy system components, and the supply chain risk management team |
| Components kept under configuration control during service and repair (SR-11(2)) | All system components, including servers, network and security devices, storage devices and end-user devices |

The first row is NIST's selection: the policy selects all three choices, and the next two rows fill in the nested values. The policy also keeps a suspected counterfeit out of the system until the investigation is complete and does not return it to the seller before then, so evidence is not lost and the part does not go back into circulation.

**Evidence assessors ask for.**

- The anti-counterfeit procedures, and the list of authorized sources
- Purchase records for a sample of installed hardware, showing an authorized source
- Receipt check records, and the settings or logs showing signature or hash verification of software and firmware
- Quarantine records and reports made for any suspected counterfeit, including to CISA or the manufacturer
- For SR-11(1), training records for receiving, IT and procurement staff (the [training record log](/templates/forms/training-record-log/))
- For SR-11(2), the [maintenance log](/templates/forms/maintenance-log/) and inventory records showing custody of components away for repair, and the check before each returned to service

**Inheritance.** Purchasing rules, receiving checks and training are usually common controls. The system owner makes sure the system's components come through those channels, that software verification is turned on in the system's tools, and that suspect components in the system are reported. Record the split in the [system security plan](/templates/plans/system-security-plan/).

**Common findings.**

- Replacement parts or memory bought from online marketplaces or brokers to save money or time, outside the authorized channel.
- Software or drivers downloaded from mirror sites with no signature or hash check.
- No reporting path: a suspect part is returned to the seller for a refund and the matter ends there.
- Receiving staff never trained to recognize counterfeit hardware or packaging.
- A component back from repair put into service without a check against its inventory record.

**Enhancements in the Moderate baseline.** Both are also in Low and High, with values in the table above.

- **[SR-11(1)](#sr-11.1)** anti-counterfeit training. NIST gives no discussion; SP 800-161 Rev. 1 says the supply chain risk management office can help find training resources and decide who needs the training. The policy makes it part of role-based training ([AT-3](/controls/at/at-3/)), before the person takes on the duties and annually thereafter; plan it in the [security and privacy training plan](/templates/plans/security-and-privacy-training-plan/).
- **[SR-11(2)](#sr-11.2)** configuration control for component service and repair. NIST gives no discussion; SP 800-161 Rev. 1 says configuration control for service and repair should be integrated into the organization's configuration control processes, and component authenticity addressed in contracts for servicing and repair. The policy records the component as "under maintenance" in the inventory, tracks it by serial number with custody in the maintenance log, inspects it for tampering ([SR-10](/controls/sr/sr-10/)) and restores its secure baseline ([CM-2](/controls/cm/cm-2/), [CM-6](/controls/cm/cm-6/)) before it returns, and requires genuine replacement parts in repair contracts. The Maintenance Policy ([MA-2](/controls/ma/ma-2/)) covers approval, sanitization and the check after repair.

SR-11(3), anti-counterfeit scanning, is in no baseline. High systems add tamper resistance and detection ([SR-9](/controls/sr/sr-9/)).
