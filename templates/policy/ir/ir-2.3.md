:::guidance
IR-2(3) is in the Privacy baseline only. NIST's discussion says that, for federal agencies, an incident that involves personally identifiable information is a breach: a loss of control, compromise, unauthorized disclosure or acquisition, or similar occurrence where someone other than an authorized user accesses or potentially accesses the information, or an authorized user does so for other than authorized purposes. The training stresses that everyone must report both confirmed and suspected breaches, in any medium or form, including paper, oral and electronic, and it includes tabletop exercises that simulate a breach. Most breaches start with an ordinary mistake, such as an email to the wrong recipient or a lost printout, so the people who need this training most are users, not only responders. The [tabletop exercise kit](/templates/plans/tabletop-exercise-kit/) has a breach scenario, and section 5.8 of the [incident handling playbook](/templates/procedures/incident-handling-playbook/) is the response the training teaches.
:::

- For a system that processes personally identifiable information, the {{org:incident-response-team}} shall provide incident response training on how to identify and respond to a breach, including the organization's process for reporting a breach. (IR-2(3))
- The training shall teach that every person must report both confirmed and suspected breaches involving information in any medium or form, including paper, oral and electronic, within the time the incident reporting statement sets (IR-6a), and shall give examples drawn from the system's own information. (IR-2(3))
- The {{org:privacy-official}} shall review the breach content of incident response training each time the content is reviewed under IR-2b, and after each breach. (IR-2(3))
- The {{org:incident-response-team}} shall include, in each annual cycle of incident response training, a tabletop exercise that simulates a breach for the personnel the incident response plan gives breach response roles. (IR-2(3))

:::federal
[OMB Memorandum M-17-12](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/legacy_drupal_files/omb/memoranda/2017/m-17-12_0.pdf), Preparing for and Responding to a Breach of Personally Identifiable Information (January 3, 2017), sets agency breach policy; OMB's FY 2026 SAOP FISMA reporting metrics (August 2026) still cite it. Section IV requires each agency to train all individuals with access to its Federal information and information systems, including detailees, contractors, grantees, volunteers and interns, on how to identify and respond to a breach, including the agency's internal reporting process, before they access Federal information or systems and in annual privacy and security awareness training. Section VI requires everyone with access to report a suspected or confirmed breach "as soon as possible and without unreasonable delay", in any medium or form, without waiting for confirmation. Section X.A requires the senior agency official for privacy to convene the breach response team for a tabletop exercise periodically, but not less than annually. [OMB Circular A-130](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/legacy_drupal_files/omb/circulars/A130/a130revised.pdf), Appendix I, section 4.f(7), also requires training and awareness for employees and contractors on how to report and respond to incidents. As of October 2026.

- Breach training shall be given to every individual with access to agency information or information systems, including detailees, contractors, grantees, volunteers and interns, before they are granted access and in the annual privacy and security awareness training, as OMB M-17-12, section IV, requires. (IR-2(3))
- The training shall teach that a suspected or confirmed breach is reported as soon as possible and without unreasonable delay, without waiting for confirmation, as OMB M-17-12, section VI, requires. (IR-2(3))
- The {{org:privacy-official}} shall convene the agency's breach response team for a breach tabletop exercise at least annually, as OMB M-17-12, section X.A, requires. (IR-2(3))

:::
