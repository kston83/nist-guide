:::guidance
IR-8(1) is in the Privacy baseline only. It adds three things to the incident response plan for breaches involving personally identifiable information: a process to decide whether to notify individuals or other organizations, including oversight bodies; an assessment of the harm, embarrassment, inconvenience or unfairness to affected individuals, with ways to reduce it; and a list of the privacy requirements that apply. NIST's discussion notes that laws, regulations and policies may set specific breach procedures, including notice, standards of harm and mitigation. [NIST SP 800-122](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/122/final) (April 2010), section 5, recommends building breach handling into the existing incident response plan, planning in advance how, when and to whom notice is given, and deciding when to offer remedial help such as credit monitoring. The [Incident Response Plan](/templates/plans/incident-response-plan/) has a breach section for these, and section 5.8 of the [incident handling playbook](/templates/procedures/incident-handling-playbook/) carries them out.
:::

- For breaches involving personally identifiable information, the incident response plan shall include a process, led by the {{org:privacy-official}}, to determine whether notice to individuals or other organizations, including oversight organizations, is needed. (IR-8(1)(a))
- The plan shall include an assessment process to determine the extent of the harm, embarrassment, inconvenience or unfairness to affected individuals, and the mechanisms available to mitigate those harms. (IR-8(1)(b))
- The plan shall identify the privacy requirements that apply to breaches, including each law, regulation, policy and contract that sets a notice, a standard of harm or a mitigation duty, with the notice each requires, to whom and by when. (IR-8(1)(c))
- The {{org:incident-response-team}} shall notify the {{org:privacy-official}} of every incident that involves, or may involve, personally identifiable information as soon as it is identified, and the {{org:privacy-official}} shall take part in its response. (IR-8(1))
- The {{org:privacy-official}} shall record each harm assessment, notification decision and mitigation decision in the incident record, with who decided and when. (IR-8(1))
- The {{org:privacy-official}} shall review the list of applicable privacy requirements each time the plan is reviewed, and when a law, regulation or contract changes. (IR-8(1)(c))

:::federal
The Privacy Act requires each agency that maintains a system of records to establish appropriate administrative, technical and physical safeguards to protect against threats or hazards to the records' security or integrity "which could result in substantial harm, embarrassment, inconvenience, or unfairness to any individual on whom information is maintained" ([5 U.S.C. § 552a(e)(10)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/5/552a?link-type=html), United States Code, 2024 edition), the standard of harm IR-8(1)(b) repeats. [OMB Circular A-130](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/legacy_drupal_files/omb/circulars/A130/a130revised.pdf), Appendix I, section 4.f(7), requires incident response capabilities and mechanisms that include notification to affected individuals; section 4.f(9) requires agencies to report incidents to OMB, DHS, the CIO, the senior agency official for privacy, their inspectors general and general counsel, law enforcement and Congress in accordance with procedures issued by OMB; and section 4.j(2)(e) requires incident response procedures for systems operated on the agency's behalf, including timelines for notification of affected individuals.

[OMB Memorandum M-17-12](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/legacy_drupal_files/omb/memoranda/2017/m-17-12_0.pdf) (January 3, 2017), which OMB's FY 2026 SAOP FISMA reporting metrics (August 2026) still cite, sets the breach procedures OMB issued. Section VII requires the senior agency official for privacy to develop and implement a breach response plan, as part of the agency's formal incident response plan, with at least these elements: the breach response team; identifying applicable privacy compliance documentation (system of records notices, privacy impact assessments and privacy notices); information sharing to respond to a breach; reporting requirements and the officials responsible; assessing the risk of harm to individuals; mitigating that risk; and notifying individuals. Section VII.A has the head of the agency designate the breach response team, led by the senior agency official for privacy and including at least the CIO or designee, the senior agency information security officer, legal counsel, and legislative affairs and communications officials; the team is always convened when a breach is a major incident. Section VII.E requires the senior agency official for privacy to conduct and document an assessment of the risk of harm, considering the nature and sensitivity of the information, the likelihood of access and use, and the type of breach. Under section VII.G, the senior agency official for privacy advises the head of the agency, who makes the final decision on whether to notify; notice is given as expeditiously as practicable and without unreasonable delay (section VII.G.2). Section VIII has the principal security operations center track and document each breach and keep the senior agency official for privacy aware of it; section IX has that center report the status of each breach to the senior agency official for privacy at the end of each quarter of the fiscal year, for that official to review and validate; and section X.B requires that official to review the plan at the end of each fiscal year.

[OMB Memorandum M-25-04](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/M-25-04-Fiscal-Year-2025-Guidance-on-Federal-Information-Security-and-Privacy-Management-Requirements.pdf), Fiscal Year 2025 Guidance on Federal Information Security and Privacy Management Requirements (January 15, 2025), is the most recent FISMA guidance memorandum. Section X defines a major incident, including a breach of personally identifiable information likely to result in demonstrable harm to the national security interests, foreign relations or economy of the United States, or to the public confidence, civil liberties or public health and safety of the American people, and requires a major incident determination for any unauthorized modification, deletion, exfiltration of, or access to the personally identifiable information of 100,000 or more people. Agencies report a major incident to CISA and the OMB Office of the Federal Chief Information Officer within 1 hour of determining it is major, notify the appropriate congressional committees and their Office of Inspector General within 7 days of having a reasonable basis to conclude it occurred, and, for a breach that is a major incident, supplement the congressional report within 30 days of discovering the breach. Its footnote 37 keeps the major incident analysis separate from the M-17-12 assessment of the risk of harm to individuals. As of October 2026.

- The incident response plan's breach section shall serve as the agency's breach response plan under OMB M-17-12, section VII, with each element that section lists, and the {{org:privacy-official}} shall review it at the end of each fiscal year as section X.B requires. (IR-8(1))
- The breach section shall name the breach response team the head of the agency designates under OMB M-17-12, section VII.A, the criteria for convening it, and the officials responsible for reporting a breach to CISA, law enforcement, inspectors general, general counsel and Congress (section VII.D). (IR-8(1)(a))
- The harm assessment shall follow OMB M-17-12, section VII.E, and the {{org:privacy-official}} shall advise the head of the agency on whether and when to notify individuals, as section VII.G provides. (IR-8(1)(a), IR-8(1)(b))
- The breach section shall apply the major incident criteria of OMB M-25-04, section X, including the required determination for a breach affecting 100,000 or more people, and its reporting: CISA and the OMB Office of the Federal Chief Information Officer within 1 hour of the determination, the congressional committees and the Office of Inspector General within 7 days, and a supplemental report within 30 days of discovering the breach. (IR-8(1)(a))
- The plan's list of applicable privacy requirements shall include the Privacy Act for each system of records, OMB M-17-12 and OMB M-25-04, and any other breach notification requirement that applies to the system's information, such as one for health information. (IR-8(1)(c))
- The {{org:privacy-official}} shall ensure that contracts and agreements for systems operated on the agency's behalf set timelines for reporting breaches to the agency and for notifying affected individuals, as OMB Circular A-130, Appendix I, section 4.j(2)(e), requires. (IR-8(1))
