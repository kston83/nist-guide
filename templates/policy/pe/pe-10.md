:::guidance
PE-10 is in the Moderate and High baselines. NIST's PE-10 discussion says emergency power shutoff applies mainly to facilities with concentrations of system resources, such as data centers, mainframe rooms, server rooms and areas with computer-controlled machinery. An office with no server room usually has nothing to which PE-10 applies beyond the building's own electrical controls; say so in the system security plan rather than leaving it blank. Accidental activation is the common finding: an unguarded button beside a door that someone mistakes for a door release.
:::

- The {{org:facilities-manager}} shall provide the capability of shutting off power to {{param:pe-10_odp.01}} in emergency situations. (PE-10a)
- The {{org:facilities-manager}} shall place emergency shutoff switches or devices {{param:pe-10_odp.02}} to facilitate access for authorized personnel. (PE-10b)
- The {{org:facilities-manager}} shall protect the emergency power shutoff capability from unauthorized activation, with protective covers on the switches, placement inside the access-controlled area, and an alarm or camera that records activation. (PE-10c)
- Staff who work in those areas shall be shown the shutoff locations and told when to use them. (PE-10b)
