:::guidance
PE-11 is in the Moderate and High baselines. NIST's PE-11 discussion describes an uninterruptible power supply (UPS) as near-instantaneous protection from power interruptions, from batteries, supercapacitors or flywheels, with a short duration that gives time to start a standby source such as a generator or to shut the system down properly. Choose the selection from the [business impact analysis](/templates/reports/business-impact-analysis/): if the system's recovery time objective tolerates waiting for power to return, an orderly shutdown is enough; if not, the UPS must carry the load until long-term alternate power takes over, and the [contingency plan](/templates/plans/contingency-plan/) should say how. High systems add PE-11(1). For a system hosted by a provider, PE-11 is inherited.
:::

- The {{org:facilities-manager}} shall provide an uninterruptible power supply to facilitate {{param:pe-11_odp}} in the event of a primary power source loss. (PE-11)
- Where the contingency plan's recovery time objective cannot be met by restarting the system after primary power returns, the {{org:facilities-manager}} shall also provide long-term alternate power, such as a generator, to which the uninterruptible power supply transitions the system. (PE-11)
- The {{org:system-owner}} shall configure the system's components to shut down in order, automatically where they support it, before the uninterruptible power supply's batteries are exhausted. (PE-11)
- The {{org:facilities-manager}} shall test the uninterruptible power supply and any generator at least annually, replace batteries as the manufacturer specifies, and confirm the runtime still covers the load. (PE-11)
