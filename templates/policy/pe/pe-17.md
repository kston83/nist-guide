:::guidance
PE-17 is in the Moderate and High baselines. NIST's PE-17 discussion says alternate work sites include government facilities and employees' private residences; that they are distinct from alternate processing sites but can serve as alternate locations during contingency operations; and that organizations can set different controls for different types of site. The controls here should match the [Rules of Behavior](/templates/forms/rules-of-behavior/), which every user signs, and the remote access requirements of the Access Control Policy (AC-17). [NIST SP 800-46 Rev. 2](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/46/r2/final) (July 2016), Guide to Enterprise Telework, Remote Access, and Bring Your Own Device (BYOD) Security, covers securing telework and remote access; Rev. 2 is the final version; a Rev. 3 pre-draft call for comments closed in October 2020, and csrc.nist.gov lists no later draft as of October 2026. Assessing effectiveness at homes is usually done through a self-assessment checklist and the endpoint management data for the devices used there, not site visits.
:::

- The {{org:ciso}} shall determine and document {{param:pe-17_odp.01}} as the alternate work sites allowed for use by employees, and the {{org:supervisor}} shall approve each employee's use of them. (PE-17a)
- The following controls shall be employed at alternate work sites: {{param:pe-17_odp.02}}. (PE-17b)
- The {{org:ciso}} shall assess the effectiveness of controls at alternate work sites at least annually, through an employee self-assessment and the device compliance data from endpoint management, and act on the gaps found. (PE-17c)
- The {{org:ciso}} shall provide a means for employees at alternate work sites to communicate with information security and privacy personnel in case of incidents, available at all hours, and employees shall report incidents through it as the Rules of Behavior require (IR-6). (PE-17d)

:::federal
Under [5 U.S.C. § 6502](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/5/6502?link-type=html), each executive agency's telework policy must require a written agreement between an agency manager and each employee authorized to telework, outlining the specific work arrangement, which is mandatory for any employee to telework (subsection (b)(2)); must be part of the agency's continuity of operations plans (subsection (b)(5)); and must enumerate when employees may temporarily work from approved overseas locations (subsection (b)(6)). Text checked in the United States Code, 2024 edition, as of October 2026.

- No employee shall telework without the written telework agreement 5 U.S.C. § 6502(b)(2) requires, and the agreement shall reference the alternate work site controls in this policy. (PE-17a)
- Work from an overseas location shall be allowed only in the circumstances the agency's telework policy enumerates under 5 U.S.C. § 6502(b)(6). (PE-17a)

:::
