:::guidance
The rule comes from the U.S. Privacy Act (5 U.S.C. § 552a(e)(7)), which protects records of how people exercise freedoms such as religion, speech, the press, assembly and petition. It is a sound rule for any organization: information about what people say, believe or join is rarely needed and easily misused. PT-7(2) is in the Privacy baseline only.
:::

- The {{org:system-owner}} shall not process information describing how any individual exercises rights guaranteed by the First Amendment, unless expressly authorized by statute or by the individual, or unless pertinent to and within the scope of an authorized law enforcement activity. (PT-7(2))

:::federal
The Privacy Act requires each agency to "maintain no record describing how any individual exercises rights guaranteed by the First Amendment unless expressly authorized by statute or by the individual about whom the record is maintained or unless pertinent to and within the scope of an authorized law enforcement activity" ([5 U.S.C. § 552a(e)(7)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title5-section552a&num=0&edition=prelim)). The general exemptions in subsection (j) cannot remove this requirement. As of September 2026.

- The {{org:privacy-official}} shall ensure no system of records holds a record of how an individual exercises First Amendment rights except as 5 U.S.C. § 552a(e)(7) allows. (PT-7(2))

:::
