:::guidance
Developer testing finds flaws before the system reaches the assessor or the adversary. NIST's SA-11 discussion names the approaches: manual code review, security architecture review, penetration testing, and static, dynamic and binary analysis, with instrumentation and fuzzing. It defines depth as the rigor and detail of testing and coverage as the number and type of artifacts tested, and has contracts set acceptance criteria for the plans, the flaw remediation process and the evidence. NIST's Secure Software Development Framework, SP 800-218 ([February 2022](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/218/final), version 1.1; as of September 2026), cites SA-11 for practices PW.7, reviewing and analyzing human-readable code, and PW.8, testing executable code.
:::

- The {{org:system-owner}} shall require the developer of the system, system component or system service, at all post-design stages of the system development life cycle, to develop and implement a plan for ongoing security and privacy control assessments. (SA-11a)
- The {{org:system-owner}} shall require the developer to perform {{param:sa-11_odp.01}} testing and evaluation {{param:sa-11_odp.02}} at {{param:sa-11_odp.03}}. (SA-11b)
- The {{org:system-owner}} shall require the developer to produce evidence of the execution of the assessment plan and of the results of the testing and evaluation. (SA-11c)
- The {{org:system-owner}} shall require the developer to implement a verifiable flaw remediation process. (SA-11d)
- The {{org:system-owner}} shall require the developer to correct flaws identified during testing and evaluation, and shall not accept a release with an unresolved flaw unless the risk is accepted (RA-7). (SA-11e)
- For a system that processes personally identifiable information, the {{org:system-owner}} shall ensure the developer's tests use test data prepared under SI-12(2), not live personally identifiable information, unless the {{org:privacy-official}} approves otherwise. (SA-11b)
