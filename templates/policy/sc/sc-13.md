:::guidance
SC-13 does not require cryptography by itself; SC-8, SC-28, IA-5 and AC-17 do. It sets the standard that cryptography must meet wherever those controls call for it. The cryptographic uses match those under configuration management in CM-3(6).
:::

- The {{org:ciso}} shall determine the cryptographic uses for the organization's systems, which include {{param:sc-13_odp.01}}. (SC-13a)
- The {{org:system-owner}} shall implement the following types of cryptography for each cryptographic use: {{param:sc-13_odp.02}}. (SC-13b)

:::federal
[FIPS 140-3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/140-3/final) (March 22, 2019) "is applicable to all Federal agencies that use cryptography-based security systems to protect sensitive information", and modules validated under NIST's Cryptographic Module Validation Program (CMVP) are considered to conform to it (sections 6 and 9). The CMVP's [FIPS 140-3 transition page](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/fips-140-3-transition-effort) sets the schedule for moving FIPS 140-2 validations to the Historical List in September 2026; modules on the Historical List remain supported for existing systems, but the standard notes that the Historical List "should not be used for procurement decisions" (as of September 2026).

- The {{org:system-owner}} shall use cryptographic modules validated by the CMVP wherever the system uses cryptography to protect sensitive information. (SC-13b)
- The {{org:system-owner}} shall select FIPS 140-3 validated modules for new systems and new purchases. (SC-13b)

:::
