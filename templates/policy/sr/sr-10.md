:::guidance
NIST's SR-10 discussion says inspection addresses physical and logical tampering and applies to systems and components removed from organization-controlled areas. It names the indications of a need for inspection: changes in packaging, specifications, factory location or the entity the part is bought from, and individuals returning from travel to high-risk locations. SP 800-161 Rev. 1 says to inspect critical systems and components at a minimum, before use and periodically after, and to put inspection requirements in contracts with suppliers, integrators and service providers, flowed down to subcontractors. The receipt check in the Physical and Environmental Protection Policy (PE-16) is the first inspection of every arriving component; the [component inventory](/templates/forms/component-inventory/) records the result.
:::

- The {{org:system-owner}} shall inspect {{param:sr-10_odp.01}} {{param:sr-10_odp.02}} to detect tampering. (SR-10)
- Each inspection shall check for physical tampering, such as broken seals, altered packaging, missing or mismatched labels and serial numbers, and signs a case was opened, and for logical tampering, such as firmware or software that does not match the version and the hash or signature the manufacturer publishes. (SR-10)
- Each inspection shall be recorded with the component, the date, who inspected it and the result. (SR-10)
- A component that shows signs of tampering shall be kept out of service and reported under the incident response plan (IR-6). (SR-10)
- Contracts with the suppliers, integrators and service providers of critical components shall require them to inspect those components for tampering, and to flow the requirement down to their subcontractors. (SR-10)
