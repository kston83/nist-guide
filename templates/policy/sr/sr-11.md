:::guidance
NIST's SR-11 discussion names manufacturers, developers, vendors and contractors as sources of counterfeit components, says anti-counterfeit policy and procedures support tamper resistance and protect against malicious code, and names CISA as an external reporting organization. SP 800-161 Rev. 1 says the policy and procedures need input from acquisition, information technology, security, legal and the supply chain risk management team, and should address contract clauses and counterfeit reporting processes, such as the Government-Industry Data Exchange Program (GIDEP), and, where appropriate, qualified bidders or manufacturers lists. The statements below are the anti-counterfeit policy; the procedure sets out the checks for each type of component. SR-11(3), anti-counterfeit scanning, is in no baseline.
:::

- The {{org:ciso}} shall develop and implement anti-counterfeit procedures that include the means to detect and prevent counterfeit components from entering the system. (SR-11a)
- Hardware, software and firmware shall be obtained only from the original manufacturer, its authorized distributors or resellers, or a source the supply chain risk management team has assessed and approved. (SR-11a)
- Each component shall be checked on receipt against the order and the manufacturer's identifiers, such as part and serial numbers, labels and packaging. (SR-11a)
- Software, firmware and updates shall be verified by digital signature or the manufacturer's published hash before installation. (SR-11a)
- A suspected counterfeit component shall be quarantined and kept out of the system until the investigation is complete, and shall not be returned to the seller before then. (SR-11a)
- A counterfeit component found installed in a system shall be handled as an incident under the incident response plan. (SR-11a)
- The {{org:ciso}} shall report counterfeit system components to {{param:sr-11_odp.01}}. (SR-11b)
