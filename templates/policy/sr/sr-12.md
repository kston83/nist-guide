:::guidance
NIST's SR-12 discussion says disposal can happen at any point in the life cycle, not only at retirement, and includes methods such as disk cleaning, removal of cryptographic keys and partial reuse of components. Disposal exposes paper and digital documentation, shipping and delivery records, memory sticks with code, and whole routers or servers with permanent media, and proper disposal keeps components out of the gray market. Sanitization itself follows the Media Protection Policy (MP-6); record it in the [media sanitization record](/templates/forms/media-sanitization-record/), and keep the disposed component in the [component inventory](/templates/forms/component-inventory/) with the status "disposed".
:::

- The {{org:system-owner}} shall dispose of {{param:sr-12_odp.01}} using the following techniques and methods: {{param:sr-12_odp.02}}. (SR-12)
- These techniques and methods shall apply whenever disposal happens in the life cycle, including during development, testing and maintenance. (SR-12)
- Each disposal of a component shall be recorded with the component's identifiers, the method, who performed and verified it, the date, and the media sanitization record or the provider's certificate. (SR-12)
- A component shall not be resold, donated or returned to a lessor or vendor unless its storage has been purged or destroyed as the Media Protection Policy requires and the {{org:system-owner}} has approved its release. (SR-12)
