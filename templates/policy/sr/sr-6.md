:::guidance
NIST's SR-6 discussion lists what a supplier review covers: security and supply chain risk management processes; foreign ownership, control or influence; and the supplier's ability to assess its own second- and third-tier suppliers. Reviews consider documented processes and controls, all-source intelligence and publicly available information, and may be done by the organization or an independent third party. SP 800-161 Rev. 1 adds that assessments should apply a consistent set of core factors and criteria, so suppliers can be compared with each other and over time, and that the sources of information should be documented; its Appendix D.4 is a sample supply chain risk assessment template. For a cloud or other external service, the [external service review](/templates/forms/external-service-review/) (SA-9) and the provider's independent attestation supply much of the evidence. A supplier assessment questionnaire is planned for this kit.
:::

- The {{org:ciso}} shall assess and review the supply chain-related risks associated with suppliers or contractors and the systems, system components or system services they provide {{param:sr-06_odp}}. (SR-6)
- Each assessment shall cover the supplier's security and supply chain risk management processes, its foreign ownership, control or influence, its ability to assess its own second- and third-tier suppliers, its documented processes and controls, and publicly available information about it. (SR-6)
- The {{org:ciso}} shall apply the same core risk factors and criteria to every supplier assessment, and record the sources of information each one relied on. (SR-6)
- A contract for a critical component or service shall not be awarded or renewed until its supplier assessment is complete and each risk found is accepted or has a planned response. (SR-6)
- The {{org:ciso}} shall have the supply chain risk management team monitor publicly available information about the suppliers of critical components and services for indications of stolen information, poor development or quality control practices, information spillage or counterfeits. (SR-6)
- The {{org:ciso}} shall share assessment and review results with other organizations only as applicable rules, policies, agreements or contracts allow or require. (SR-6)

:::federal
[41 U.S.C. § 1326](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/41/1326?link-type=html)(a) makes the head of each executive agency responsible for assessing the supply chain risk posed by the acquisition and use of covered articles, and for prioritizing those assessments based on the criticality of the mission, system, component, service or asset. [NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/161/r1/upd1/final), Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management Practices for Systems and Organizations (May 2022, updated November 1, 2024; current as of October 2026), Appendix E, gives federal agencies baseline risk factors (section E.2.2) to address, at a minimum, for assessments of critical sources and covered articles, and says (section E.2.1) that determining assessment priorities, evaluating impact and making risk response decisions are inherently governmental functions that cannot be outsourced, although a qualified, vetted third party may support research and documentation. Under [41 CFR 201-1.201(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-41/section-201-1.201), an executive agency must expeditiously submit supply chain risk information to the Federal Acquisition Security Council's information sharing agency when the Council requests it, or when the agency determines there is a reasonable basis to conclude that a substantial supply chain risk exists in connection with a source or covered article. Statute checked in the United States Code, 2024 edition, and regulation in the eCFR, as of October 2026.

- The {{org:ciso}} shall prioritize supplier assessments by the criticality of the mission, system, component, service or asset, as 41 U.S.C. § 1326(a)(2) requires. (SR-6)
- Assessments of critical sources and covered articles shall address every baseline risk factor in NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1, Appendix E, section E.2.2. (SR-6)
- Agency officials shall set assessment priorities and make each risk response decision; a contractor may support the research and documentation but shall not make those decisions. (SR-6)
- When the agency determines there is a reasonable basis to conclude that a substantial supply chain risk exists in connection with a source or covered article, the {{org:ciso}} shall submit the relevant supply chain risk information to the Federal Acquisition Security Council's information sharing agency, as 41 CFR 201-1.201(b) requires. (SR-6)

:::
